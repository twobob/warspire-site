Matchmaking and lobbies

Finding a match, the lobby wait, bots and rejoining.

A match of Warspire always has 8 players. You do not pick opponents or a room: you press Find Match, the game puts you in a lobby with whoever else is looking, and bots take every seat that no person fills. This page follows that from the title screen to the next match. For what happens once the match is running, see How a match works.

Finding a match

On the title screen, type a name (or play under your Steam name when Steam is running) and press Find Match. A name can be up to 24 characters long. If someone in the lobby already uses yours, a number in brackets is added to it. The button reads Reconnect instead when you still hold a seat in a match that is running: see disconnecting below.

The game then looks at every lobby that has not started and still has a seat free for a person, and puts you in the one that already holds the most people. Lobbies therefore fill up one at a time, and a new lobby opens only when every other one is full or already playing. You never join a match that has started.

Many matches run side by side, each with its own lobby, clock and players: up to 64 at once, lobbies included. If that limit is reached and no lobby has room, Find Match tells you the game is busy and you can try again shortly. The public game accepts 512 connections at a time. When it is full the title screen says so and tries again on its own.

The lobby wait

A lobby starts its match 20 seconds after the latest player joined it. Every new player restarts that wait from the top, so a lobby that keeps attracting people keeps waiting for more. A player who leaves does not restart it.

The moment all 8 seats hold people, the match starts at once, with no wait left to run.

When the wait runs out with seats still empty, the match starts anyway and bots play the empty seats. A lobby with one person in it becomes a match of one person and 7 bots.

In the public game the lobby always waits: there is no Ready button, and nothing a player does starts the match early. A privately hosted game played from the desktop build does show a Ready button in the lobby, and there the match also starts as soon as every connected person in the lobby is ready.

What the lobby screen shows

While you wait, the title screen turns into the lobby. You cannot shop, level or place units yet: the first preparation phase begins when the match does.

  • A countdown: "the match starts in" so many seconds. It jumps back up whenever someone joins.
  • A line counting the people and the bots in the lobby. This is the line that tells you how many people you are waiting with.
  • 8 seats. Yours comes first and is marked YOU, then the other people in name order, then the bots.
  • A mark under each name: IN LOBBY for a person, BOT for a computer player. Where a Ready button is offered, people show READY or NOT READY instead.
  • The heading, which shows seats taken out of 8. Bots count as seated, and they fill the lobby from the moment it opens, so the heading reads full from the start and says nothing about how many people have joined.

Leaving a lobby

To leave a lobby, open the menu and press Quit Match, or simply close the game. Either way your seat goes straight back to a bot and nothing is held for you: a lobby seat cannot be resumed. The other players' countdown carries on as it was.

If the last person leaves, the lobby is cleared and starts again from nothing for whoever comes next.

If your connection drops while you are in a lobby, the game reconnects and joins a lobby again on its own. That counts as a new player joining, so the wait restarts.

Disconnecting during a match

Once the match has started, your seat is yours until you are knocked out or you quit. Losing your connection, closing the tab or putting your phone away does not give it up.

  • Your army stays exactly as you left it and fights every round. Rounds do not wait for you: the other players' ready marks decide when combat starts, as if you were not there.
  • You still earn gold and experience each round, your shop still refreshes, and you still take damage when you lose. Nobody spends your gold for you.
  • With automatic deployment switched on (it is on unless you turned it off), units waiting on your bench are moved to free board cells at the start of each fight, strongest first, up to your unit cap.
  • Choices that cannot wait are made for you when the fight starts: an open loot offer takes one of its items at random, an open commander offer takes the first commander and build shown, and open talent offers take their first option.

To return, do nothing: the game tries to reconnect by itself, up to 8 times, waiting a little longer each time (never more than 15 seconds). If it gives up, or you closed the game, open it again on the same device and browser and press Reconnect. Your seat is remembered there, not by your name, so typing the same name on another device starts a new match instead.

You come back to the match as it is now: the current round, your gold, your board. If a fight is under way you join it part way through. Opening the game a second time while the first is still connected moves your seat to the newer one and closes the older.

A match needs someone watching. When every person still alive in it has been disconnected for 15 seconds, the rest of the match is played out at full speed with no timers. Reconnect before it finishes and it drops back to normal pace with you in it. If it finishes with nobody connected, the match closes, and Reconnect then tells you it is no longer available and offers a new one.

Quitting a match

Quit Match in the menu (two taps, so it cannot happen by accident) is a forfeit. Your health drops to zero and you cannot come back to that match. See Player damage and elimination for how placements are decided.

  • During preparation you are knocked out on the spot and take the lowest placement still open.
  • During combat your army finishes the fight it is in, so your opponent is not left without one. You are then knocked out with anyone else who falls that round.
  • If that leaves one player standing, the match ends and that player wins.

You go back to the title screen, where Find Match puts you in a new lobby. Quitting is different from disconnecting: a disconnected player keeps the seat, a player who quits gives it up.

Playing again

Once you are knocked out, or the match ends, the results screen offers Play Again. It works exactly like Find Match: you leave the old match and take a seat in the fullest open lobby, under the same name. A player who is still alive in a running match cannot move on this way; that takes Quit Match.

You do not wait for the bots to finish. As soon as no person is left alive in a match, because the last one was knocked out or quit, the bots play out every remaining round at full speed with no timers. A fight that is already being shown ends at its normal time first. The final standings then appear at once, and the results stay open for 45 seconds from that moment.

A finished match stays open for 45 seconds so everyone can read the standings and statistics. After that, or sooner if every person has left, it closes. Anyone still on the results screen is told the match has closed, and Play Again still works.

A seat in a finished match is not kept once the match closes. The closed match makes way for a fresh lobby.
