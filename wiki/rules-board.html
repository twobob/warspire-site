Board and bench

The board, the bench, placement and the unit cap.

Your warband lives in two places. The board is a grid 8 cells wide and 4 rows deep, 32 cells in all, and only units standing on it fight. The bench is a single row of 8 slots for units you are holding back. This page covers how units get onto each, how many may fight, and what you may move while a fight is running. How the fight itself runs is on Combat.

The board

The row nearest the enemy is your front row and the row nearest you is your back row. You may put a unit on any empty cell of your 32. Nothing about a cell is special in itself: what matters is how far it is from the enemy and from your other units.

For a fight the two players' halves are joined front row to front row into one field 8 cells wide and 8 rows deep. The opponent's half is turned half a turn, so their left flank stands opposite your right flank and their front row touches yours. Units may walk anywhere on the joined field once the fight starts.

The bench

12345678

The bench holds up to 8 units. Benched units do not fight, do not count toward alliances and are not limited by the unit cap. They do count when the game looks for 3 copies to combine (see Star upgrades), and an item may be put on a benched unit ready for later.

The unit cap

The unit cap is the number of units that may fight for you. It equals your player level, and is never less than 3, so the first levels all field 3 units. Levels are explained on Levels and experience.

Buying onto the bench or the board

  • A unit bought from the shop goes to the first free bench slot unless you drop it somewhere else. You may drop it on a chosen bench slot or straight onto a board cell.
  • The slot you choose must be empty. The one exception: if the new unit is the last copy needed for a combine (3 of the same unit and star), the purchase goes through and the unit merges at once.
  • With a full bench you can still buy a unit that completes a combine, because the merged unit takes the place of one of the copies. Any other purchase is refused until a slot is free.
  • Buying is allowed at any time in a round, also while a fight is running. During a fight the unit can only go to the bench until your own fight has ended (see below).
  • You cannot buy a unit you already own at the highest star (3).
  • Buying straight onto the board is allowed even when the board is already at the unit cap. The extra unit is dealt with when the fight starts (see the over cap rules below).

Moving, swapping and selling

  • Drag a unit to any empty cell or slot to move it: board to board, bench to bench, bench to board or board to bench.
  • Drop a unit on an occupied cell or slot and the two units swap places. This works between the board and the bench as well.
  • Selling works from the board or the bench. The gold returned is on Star upgrades and the copies go back to the shared pool. An item on a sold unit returns to your inventory.
  • 3 copies of a unit at the same star merge wherever they stand, on the board or the bench, as soon as you buy or move any unit. The only delay is while your board is locked in a fight (see what you may do during combat).
  • During preparation you may place more units on the board than the cap allows. This lets you swap and compare freely. Only the cap at the moment the fight starts matters.
  • The commander moves between board cells like any unit, and may swap with a board unit, but can never go to the bench and can never be sold.

Auto deploy

Auto deploy fills empty places on your board from the bench when the fight starts. It is on when the match begins and you may switch it at any time.

  • It runs once, at the moment preparation ends, and only while the board holds fewer units than the cap.
  • Benched units are taken strongest first. Strength is the unit's tier plus 2 for each star above the first; between equals the unit in the lower bench slot goes first.
  • A melee unit (attack range of one cell) is put in the front row, a ranged unit in the back row. Within a row the centre columns fill first, and of two equally central columns the left one. When the row is full the next row is used. The grids at the top of this page show the exact order.
  • It never moves a unit that is already on the board and never removes anything.

A unit bought straight onto the bench therefore still fights if there is room. Switch auto deploy off if you keep units on the bench on purpose, for example copies saved for a combine.

Over the cap when the fight starts

When preparation ends, auto deploy runs first (if it is on) and then the board is brought down to the unit cap. A board that is at or under the cap is left alone.

  • Units are sent back to the bench one at a time, starting with the unit you touched most recently. Touching means buying it, moving or swapping it, or changing its item. Each goes to the first free bench slot.
  • If the bench fills up before the board is down to the cap, the remaining extra units are sold. The cheapest sell value goes first, and between equals the most recently touched. You receive the normal sell gold, the copies return to the shared pool and any item returns to your inventory.
  • The commander is never benched or sold by this rule.
The newest arrival is the first to leave. If you want a new unit to fight, make room for it yourself by taking another unit off the board.

What you may do during combat

When the fight starts your board is locked. It unlocks as soon as your own fight is over, even while other players are still fighting, and stays unlocked until the next fight starts. The phases and timers are on Rounds and phases.

  • Nothing you change during a round alters the fight in progress. The fight is decided from your board as it stood when preparation ended; changes count from the next fight.
  • While the board is locked, combines only use copies on the bench. A third copy bought while its partners are fighting waits on the bench. It merges the next time you buy or move a unit after the board has unlocked, and at the latest when the next preparation starts.
  • A player who has been eliminated can do none of this.

The commander's place

  • When you choose a commander in round 10 it is placed on the board for you, by the same rule as auto deploy: a melee commander in the front row, a ranged commander in the back row, centre first.
  • If every cell of the board is taken, your weakest board unit moves to the bench to make room, or is sold if the bench is full as well.
  • The commander takes one board cell, does not count toward the unit cap, does not count toward any alliance, cannot be benched and cannot be sold. You may move it to any board cell you like.
  • The commander may hold an item, except items that only work on a unit with a mana ability.

Everything else about commanders is on How commanders work.

Positioning

These follow directly from the targeting rules on Combat.

  • A unit attacks the nearest enemy, counting a diagonal step as one cell. Your front row is nearest, so it is attacked first. Put the units that can take it in front and fragile ranged units behind them.
  • Between two enemies at the same distance, a melee unit prefers the one straight ahead or straight beside it over a diagonal one, and after that every unit prefers the enemy with less health. A wounded unit standing level with a healthy one draws the attacks.
  • A unit keeps its target for as long as it can still reach it. The first enemy to come near decides whom your unit fights, so the column you place a unit in decides its first opponent.
  • The enemy half is turned half a turn. A unit in your left corner faces the enemy's right corner, which is column 8 as they see it. Check their layout on the scoreboard with that in mind.
  • A corner cell has fewer neighbours than a centre cell, so fewer melee attackers can stand around a unit there.
  • A summon appears in the free cell nearest its creator and a placeable next to the unit that holds its item (see Summons and placeables). You do not choose which of the touching cells is taken, and the order differs from fight to fight, so do not count on a wall landing in front of its holder. If one cell matters, fill the other cells around the holder with your own units.
  • Abilities choose targets by their own rules, listed on Triggers and targeting. Many hit an area, so standing units shoulder to shoulder has a cost.
