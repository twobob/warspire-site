Damage, armour and resistance

Damage types and how armour and magic resistance reduce them.

Every point of damage in Warspire has one of three types. The type decides which defence of the target reduces it: armour against physical damage, magic resistance against magical damage, and nothing against pure damage. This page follows a hit from the attacker's roll to the target's health bar. To try your own numbers, use the Damage calculator.

Shields absorb all three types. Evasion only works against basic attacks: abilities and damage over time cannot be dodged.

Armour

Armour reduces physical damage. The physical damage a unit takes is multiplied by:

1 - (0.052 × armour) / (0.9 + 0.048 × |armour|)

where |armour| is the armour value without its sign. The first point of armour removes 5.5% of physical damage. Each further point removes a little less than the one before, but every point adds about the same amount of effective health against physical damage, so armour never stops being worth having.

Armour can go below zero when enemies strip it. Negative armour makes the unit take more physical damage, following the same curve mirrored, up to a limit of 108% more.

At 225 armour the multiplier reaches zero and physical damage is reduced to nothing. Damage is never negative, so more armour than that changes nothing.

Magic resistance

Magic resistance is a plain percentage. The magical damage a unit takes is multiplied by:

1 - magic resistance / 100

So 25 magic resistance removes 25% of magical damage. Bonuses from different sources add together. Anything above 100 counts as 100, which is full immunity to magical damage. There is no lower limit: negative magic resistance makes the unit take that much more.

61 of the 65 units in the shop start with no magic resistance at all, so most of it comes from items, alliances and abilities, listed further down.

Damage reduction

Damage reduction is a second layer on top of armour and magic resistance. It removes a percentage of physical and magical damage after armour or magic resistance has done its work. Pure damage ignores it.

Several sources of damage reduction do not add. Each one is applied to what the others let through, so two sources of 20% give 36%, not 40%. A single source counts for at most 100%.

Some debuffs carry negative damage reduction. That works the other way: a unit with -20% takes 20% more physical and magical damage.

Full damage reduction is not invulnerability. Last Light, Swept Aloft each give 100% damage reduction. That blocks every point of physical and magical damage, but pure damage still goes through: pure hits, pure damage over time and the health drain of overtime all hurt a unit that is otherwise untouchable.

The order of a hit

A basic attack that reaches its target is worked out in this order. For when and how units attack, see Combat.

  1. Can it land? An attack on a target that has died or become untargetable misses. A projectile already in the air misses too.
  2. Evasion. The target's evasion is rolled. On a dodge the attack does nothing more, apart from effects that fire when the target evades.
  3. Damage roll. A whole number is picked at random, each equally likely, between the attacker's minimum and maximum attack damage.
  4. Flat bonuses. All bonus attack damage is added to the roll.
  5. Percentage bonuses. The sum is multiplied by 1 plus the attacker's total bonus damage percentage. Penalties can lower the result to zero but not below.
  6. Critical hit. If the attacker has a critical chance, it is rolled now. A critical hit multiplies the damage by the attacker's critical damage.
  7. Attacker's mana. The attacker gains mana from the damage as it stands now, before the target's defences.
  8. Overtime. If the fight is in overtime, the damage is raised by the current overtime bonus.
  9. Armour or magic resistance. The damage is multiplied by the armour multiplier (physical) or the magic resistance multiplier (magical). Pure damage skips this step and the next.
  10. Damage reduction. The result is multiplied by what the target's damage reduction lets through.
  11. Shields. The target's shields soak up what is left, oldest shield first.
  12. Last stand. If the remaining damage would kill a target that can survive a killing blow, it is left on 1 health instead. This works once per fight.
  13. Health. Whatever passed the shields comes off the target's health.
  14. Target's mana. The target gains mana from the damage after its defences. Damage soaked by a shield still counts here.
  15. Lifesteal. The attacker heals for its lifesteal share of the damage after defences.
  16. Triggers. Effects that fire on being damaged, on falling below a health threshold, on a landed attack, on a critical hit and on being attacked are queued, in that order. See Triggers and targeting.

Put together, for a physical attack:

damage = (roll + flat bonus) × (1 + bonus% / 100) × critical × overtime × armour multiplier × (1 - damage reduction)

Ability damage is simpler. An ability, an item effect or an alliance effect deals the amount it states (some add a share of the target's or the caster's health). It starts at the overtime step above. It cannot be dodged, cannot be a critical hit, heals nothing through lifesteal, and the caster's bonus attack damage and bonus damage percentage are not added to it.

There is no minimum damage: enough armour, magic resistance or damage reduction brings a hit down to zero. A hit of zero gives the target no mana and fires no effects that react to damage, though the attacker still gains its mana for the swing. There is also no cap at the target's remaining health. A hit that kills counts its full damage after defences for lifesteal and for the damage totals shown after the match.

Critical hits

Only basic attacks can be critical hits. A unit has no critical chance of its own. It gets one from items, alliances or abilities, and chances from several sources add together, up to 100%.

Critical damage does not add up. When a unit has several sources, only the highest multiplier is used. A unit with a critical chance but no multiplier of its own deals 200% damage on a critical hit.

The critical multiplier is applied after flat and percentage bonuses and before the target's defences, so it scales with every damage bonus the attacker has.

Sources of critical chance include Keen Whetstone, Stormglass Shard, Twin Fang Daggers, Executioner's Glaive, Worldsplitter Axe, Cutthroat. The full list is under critical chance.

Lifesteal and healing

Lifesteal heals the attacker for a percentage of the damage its basic attacks deal after the target's defences. Damage absorbed by a shield still counts, and so does damage beyond the target's remaining health. Ability damage and damage over time never steal life. Lifesteal from several sources adds together.

Healing from abilities, health regeneration and lifesteal all goes through the same steps:

health restored = heal × (1 + healing received% / 100) × (1 - overtime healing penalty)

Healing received is the sum of every bonus and penalty on the unit being healed. It cannot go below -100%, at which point the unit cannot be healed at all. A heal never raises health above the unit's maximum, and the part that would is lost.

Effects that cut healing: Wounded (Cutthroat, 3 units) (-75% healing received), Wounded (Cutthroat, 6 units) (-100% healing received).

In overtime, which starts 35 seconds into a fight, every heal is reduced. The penalty starts at 10% and grows by 10% every second, up to 80%. This multiplies with the unit's own healing received.

Health regeneration (see the stat) is a heal like any other, paid out every 0.5 seconds.

When a unit's maximum health goes up during a fight, its current health goes up by the same amount. When the bonus ends, current health only drops if it is above the new maximum.

Shields

A shield is extra health that sits in front of the health bar. It absorbs damage of every type, pure damage included, after armour, magic resistance and damage reduction have been applied, so a shield on a well armoured unit goes further.

  • A unit can hold several shields at once. They are used up in the order they were gained.
  • A shield lasts until it is used up or its time runs out. A shield with no duration lasts the whole fight.
  • Shields are not healing. Healing bonuses and penalties do not change them, and they are not limited by maximum health.
  • Damage soaked by a shield still counts as damage dealt: it gives mana to both sides, feeds lifesteal and fires effects that react to damage.

Shields come from: Resonance Mantle, Sanctuary Bell, Ember Vow, Cinder Mantle, Prism Veil.

Reflection and striking back

Warspire has no reflection stat: no percentage of incoming damage is sent back automatically. Effects that punish attackers are triggers that deal a stated amount of damage when their holder is attacked or damaged. That damage is ordinary ability damage of its stated type, reduced like any other damage of that type: by the attacker's own armour if it is physical, by its magic resistance if it is magical, and not at all if it is pure.

Effects that strike back: Barbed Hauberk (15 pure damage to the attacker for each attack that lands on its holder).

Damage dealt by a trigger does not set off effects that fire when a unit is damaged, so two such effects cannot bounce damage between each other without end.

Damage over time

Damage over time is stated per second and dealt in portions every 0.5 seconds. Each portion is a separate hit of its type, reduced by the target's armour or magic resistance as they are at that moment, so stripping armour from a burning target makes a physical burn hurt more straight away.

Damage over time cannot be dodged or critical, gives the target mana, but does not fire effects that react to being damaged. How effects stack and refresh is covered in How status effects work.

Examples: Fevered Armlet, Burning Pitch, Shredded, Unravelling, Static Shock, Sentenced, Leeched, Embers.

Effective health

Effective health is how much damage of one type it takes to bring a unit down from full health. It is the fairest way to compare a unit with a lot of health and little armour against one with less health and more armour.

effective health = health / damage multiplier

Against physical damage the multiplier is the armour multiplier, against magical damage the magic resistance multiplier, and against pure damage it is 1, so effective health against pure damage is simply health. Damage reduction divides further: a unit that takes 80% of the damage after armour has its effective health divided by 0.8 again.

Worked example. Spire Colossus at one star has 3,000 health and 15 armour, which is 5,786 effective health against physical damage. With Cragscale Coat (+18 armour) that becomes 33 armour and 9,703 effective health, a gain of 3,917. With Cragscale Coat (+18 armour) that becomes 18 armour and 1,491 effective health, a gain of 791.

The gain is larger on the unit with more health, because each point of armour is worth a share of the health behind it. Armour and magic resistance are best placed on units that already have a large health pool, and extra health is best placed on units that already have strong defences.

What changes armour and resistance

Everything in Warspire that raises or lowers armour, magic resistance or damage reduction. A negative number is a penalty. Three numbers separated by slashes are the values at one, two and three stars. An alliance value is labelled with the number of units its step needs. Words after a number say when it counts: a bonus given per adjacent unit or per kill is that much for each one, up to the limit shown. A bonus an item or ability only applies for a time is listed once, under the status effect that carries it.
