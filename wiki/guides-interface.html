The interface

A tour of every panel on screen.

This is a tour of every screen in Warspire in the order you meet them, with a link from each panel to the rule it shows. How to work each control is in Controls.

Title screen

The game opens on the title screen: the Warspire title on the left, and behind it a live board in your chosen arena with a hero standing on show.

  • Your name. On Windows with Steam running you are signed in under your Steam name. Otherwise, and always in the browser, you type a name to play under.
  • Find Match looks for a match. If the connection fails the screen says why and the button becomes Retry; if you left a match that is still running it becomes Reconnect. See Matchmaking and lobbies.
  • RULEBOOK opens the in-game rulebook: how to play, units, alliances, items, commanders and a glossary.
  • SETTINGS opens the menu described below. QUIT closes the game on Windows; the browser version has no quit button.
  • HERO, ARENA and SCENERY, at the bottom right, each have an arrow on either side. They choose the hero on show, the arena you play in and its lighting. All three are saved.

Lobby

Once a match is found the screen becomes the lobby: a roster of 8 seats with portraits. The heading counts how many are seated.

  • Each seat carries a tag: YOU, IN LOBBY, READY or NOT READY, CONNECTING, or BOT.
  • An empty seat reads Open seat. A bot fills it when the match starts.
  • Cancel leaves the lobby. On servers that allow an early start a Ready button is offered as well.

The wait and what ends it are covered in Matchmaking and lobbies.

The match screen

During a match the board owns the screen. Panels have no frames: text and icons sit on soft shade along the edges, and most controls are round icon buttons. Every one has a tooltip.

Round clock

At the top centre a caption names the phase and round (PREPARATION, COMBAT or MATCH OVER, with the round number and the round type on special rounds) over a large countdown number and a thin timer line.

  • In preparation the number counts down to the fight. The last five seconds beat on the number, and a FIGHT flash marks the start of combat.
  • In combat the caption reads COMBAT and the clock counts down from the longest a fight can last (see Time limit and overtime), so it does not give away when a fight will end. Most fights finish well before it reaches zero.
  • When your own fight is over the caption counts the fights still running: 2 FIGHTS LEFT, 1 FIGHT LEFT, then ROUND ENDING.
  • When every fight is over the caption reads NEW SHOP IN and the number turns gold and counts the last 10 seconds to the next preparation.

Phases and timers: Rounds and phases.

Shop drawer

The shop is a drawer across the far half of the board, which is empty during preparation. It opens by itself at the start of every preparation and closes for combat. It offers 5 units, each standing as a live model with no card behind it.

  • Under each unit: a gold coin with its cost, its name in its tier colour, its alliance tiles, and pips counting the copies you own toward the next star (Star upgrades).
  • A unit whose purchase would complete an upgrade is washed in colour and labelled with the star it would reach. A unit you cannot afford shows a red coin; it is never dimmed.
  • Left edge: the book (the rulebook's unit list), the padlock (keep these offers for next round) and the sword (auto-deploy, see Board and bench).
  • Right edge: the cross to close (Space), the reroll button with its cost (D), and the level ring (F).
  • Closed, only the round shop button remains at the top right, carrying your gold. Its tooltip breaks down your next income (Gold and income).

Odds, rerolls and the lock: The shop and The shared pool. For exact chances use the Shop odds calculator.

Gold, level and experience

  • Gold is the number on the shop button. Beside the shop the drawer shows your win or lose streak and the gold you can expect next round. Gold and income explains each part.
  • Level is the number inside the level ring, with your experience toward the next level as a caption under it and the cost of buying more on a coin. Its tooltip lists the shop odds at your level. See Levels and experience.
  • Units: a UNITS counter shows how many units are on your board against your cap (UNITS 0/3 at the very start). The count turns red with an arrow when you are over the cap, and a warning appears in the last seconds of preparation. See Board and bench.

Board and bench

Your half of the board is the near 8 by 4 cells; the far half is the enemy's. The bench of 8 slots runs along the bottom edge.

  • The sell zone sits at the bottom left in preparation and lights up, showing the gold you would get, while a unit is selected or dragged.
  • Tapping a unit opens its details card on the right: portrait, stars, where it stands, its stats, ability and alliances, its item with UNEQUIP, and SELL with TO BOARD or TO BENCH. When a button cannot be used the card says why.
  • The selected unit is outlined on the board.

Placement and the unit cap: Board and bench. What the stats mean: Stat reference.

Unit nameplates

Every unit carries a small plate over its head: star pips, a health bar, a thinner mana bar under it, and a tiny icon of its item with a rim in the item's tier colour. The name appears only when you point at, select or drag onto the unit, so a full board stays readable. Plates never overlap: one that would touch another is nudged aside.

Health and mana in a fight: Combat and Mana and casting. Floating damage numbers can be switched off in the menu (Platforms and settings).

Players rail

Down the left edge is one row per player, under a PLAYERS heading that counts those still alive. Each row has a portrait, the name, a small line with level and gold, and a large health number. Your own row and your current opponent's are highlighted.

  • During combat a row reads FIGHTING while that player's fight is still running and pulses each time one of their units is hit. It changes to DONE when their fight is over.
  • Other marks: WATCHING or VIEWING on the board you are looking at, VS on your opponent during a fight, LAST VS on the same row in the next preparation, and OFFLINE on a player who has dropped.
  • Tap a row to watch that player's board in preparation or their fight in combat. Tap the heading for the full scoreboard (Tab).

Who you meet: Matchups. Health and knockouts: Player damage and elimination.

Scoreboard

The scoreboard is the rail in full: every player with health, level, gold and net worth, streak, last result, their record against you, commander, the units on their board with stars and items, their alliances and their items. Tapping a row watches that player; a MY BOARD button brings you home.

Right column: damage, items, alliances

The right edge stacks three lists, top to bottom.

  • DAMAGE: the five units that have dealt the most in the current fight, each with a bar and a number. The heading expands to the full list (at most 10 units), a pill switches between MINE and ENEMY, and each row's tooltip splits the figure by damage type and adds damage taken, healing and kills. It keeps the last fight until the next begins, and can be turned off in the menu. See Damage, armour and resistance.
  • ITEMS: your unequipped items as round icons (up to 12), then EQUIPPED items shown on their holders' portraits, then GLOBAL items that work for the whole army. The section is hidden while you own none. See Loot and items and Items.
  • ALLIANCES: a tile for each alliance on your board, in full colour when active and dimmed when not, with pip bars showing progress to each step. A tile's tooltip lists every step. While you watch another player the heading carries their name. See How alliances count and Alliances.

Loot offer

On a loot round SELECT AN ITEM appears over the dimmed board: 3 cards side by side, each with a round icon, a header in the item's tier colour with its name and tier, and its description. The first loot round is round 1, so this is the first choice a match asks of you.

  • A long bar under the cards counts down the time to choose, and a line shows how many players have chosen.
  • If the time runs out, one of the cards is taken for you at random when combat starts.
  • REROLL swaps the cards for new ones of the same tier, once per offer. It costs no gold.
  • SHOW BOARD hides the offer so you can check your board; PICK ITEM at the bottom right brings it back.
  • With 12 unequipped items you get no offer, so equip items before a loot round.

When loot rounds come and which tiers they offer: Loot and items.

Commander pick

You play the first rounds without a commander. At the start of round 10 the preparation opens with CHOOSE YOUR COMMANDER: four commanders on offer, each on a large card with full-body art, name, role, a short description and the passive ability. That preparation runs 10 seconds longer than usual to leave time for the choice.

  • Under each commander are its builds (BUILD I and BUILD II), each naming its core and ultimate abilities, with a PICK button.
  • A countdown in the heading shows how long you have. If it runs out, the first commander and first build are chosen for you.
  • VIEW BOARD hides the picker so you can look at your board. The pick waits for you until the timer ends.

What commanders do is in How commanders work; the list is in Commanders.

Commander card and talent picker

Once you have a commander, a status card shows its portrait, role, level and health, its abilities with their cooldowns during a fight, and a gem for each talent level (levels 4, 6, 8 and 10).

  • When a talent is ready, CHOOSE A TALENT opens with two cards for that level. Each shows the icon, name, tags, the full description and what the talent changes, with a CHOOSE button.
  • DECIDE LATER hides the picker. The gem on the card then waits; tap it (T) to reopen.
  • A talent still unchosen when combat starts is assigned for you (the first option).

See How commanders work and Talents.

Banners, versus cards and duel portraits

  • Round banner: at the start of preparation, ROUND and its number with the breakdown of the gold you just received (Gold and income).
  • Versus cards: before each fight two portrait cards face each other across crossed swords, with names, commander titles and top alliances. A ghost board is labelled as one.
  • Duel portraits: for the whole fight you stand in the bottom left corner and your opponent in the bottom right, with name, level, gold and health.
  • Result plate: VICTORY, DEFEAT, DRAW or TIME UP in large letters over the far half of the board, with who beat whom and the damage dealt to the loser (Player damage and elimination).
  • Messages: short notes appear under the clock, up to three at a time, for things such as a refused action or your board unlocking. They never block a tap.

Watching another player

While you watch someone else the rail marks their row, the alliances heading carries their name, their units can be inspected but not touched, and a MY BOARD button returns you (Space). Eliminated players can keep watching the same way.

Menu

The gear at the top right (Esc) opens the menu, in five tabs. It is the same menu as SETTINGS on the title screen.

  • AUDIO: master, music and effects volume.
  • VIDEO: display and graphics quality.
  • GAME: damage numbers, the damage meter, and hiding inactive alliances during combat.
  • ACCESSIBILITY: colour-blind mode with a preview of the palette.
  • CONTROLS: the key bindings screen.

Along the bottom: RESUME, RULEBOOK, QUIT MATCH (back to the title screen; the match goes on without you) and, on Windows, QUIT GAME. Both quit buttons ask for a second tap. Every option is explained in Platforms and settings.

Rulebook

The in-game rulebook (C) opens over anything, from the title screen, the menu or the book button on the shop. Its tabs cover how to play, units by tier with a page for each, alliances, items by tier, commanders with their builds and talents, and a glossary. It reads the same game data as this wiki, so the two always agree. The wiki's own lists start at Units, Alliances, Items and Glossary.

Results screen

When you are knocked out, or the match ends, the results screen opens with your finish in large letters: VICTORY, your place, or ELIMINATED.

  • A table lists every player in finishing order with damage dealt, gold earned, rerolls and their best units. Column headings have tooltips.
  • A round-by-round strip shows your match: one bar per round, coloured for win, loss or draw, its height your health after that round. Each bar's tooltip gives the opponent, damage both ways, your level and your gold.
  • SHARE buttons open a share page with your result filled in; nothing is posted until you confirm there. SAVE IMAGE and COPY TEXT do what they say.
  • KEEP WATCHING returns to the match as a spectator while it is still running; CONTINUE goes back to the title screen.

How places are decided and what is recorded: After the match and Player damage and elimination.
