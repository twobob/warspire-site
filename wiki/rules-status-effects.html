How status effects work

Durations, stacking, buffs, debuffs and disables.

A status effect is anything that changes a unit for a while: more attack speed, less armour, a stun, a burn. Each one is a small bundle of stat changes with a name, held by one unit. The game has 100 named effects, listed on the status effects page; many abilities, items and alliances also carry unnamed bonuses that follow exactly the same rules. This page explains those rules. What applies an effect, and to whom, is covered in Triggers and targeting.

Buffs, debuffs and disables

Every effect is sorted into a class by what it carries, not by who applied it.

  • Debuff: the effect carries a stun, silence, root or disarm, or damage over time, or any stat change with a negative value (less armour, slower attacks, less healing received). One harmful part is enough: an effect that adds damage but removes armour is a debuff.
  • Disable: a debuff that carries a stun, silence, disarm or root. Disables are the debuffs that stop a unit from acting.
  • Buff: everything else. That includes being untargetable, healing over time, and protection from death.

The class matters for one thing: dispelling. A dispel removes either the buffs or the debuffs of its target. Of the named effects, 51 are buffs, 34 are debuffs without a disable and 15 are disables.

The family is the most important thing the effect carries; an effect that stuns and also burns is counted once, as a stun. Browse them all on the status effects page.

Because the class looks only at what an effect carries, an effect that a unit puts on itself or on its allies can still be a debuff. A friendly dispel strips it like any other debuff, and so does a revive. That is welcome when the effect is a drawback and unwelcome when it is a protection.

  • Pact Silence, from Abyssal (cannot cast abilities): a debuff because it carries a silence.
  • Last Light, from Lastlight Disc (100% less physical and magical damage taken, cannot attack, cannot be targeted by enemies): a debuff because it carries a disarm.

The dispels that can end these early: Purging Light, Smoke and Mirrors, Nullfire Mantle.

Timed effects and lasting effects

An effect reaches a unit in one of two ways, and the two behave differently.

  • Timed effects are put on a unit during the fight by an ability, an item or a trigger (the apply a status effect effect). They have a duration, they can be refreshed and stacked, and they can be dispelled.
  • Lasting effects are fixed when the fight is set up: the bonuses of an active alliance step, of an equipped item, of an ace, of a global item, and of an aura. They last the whole fight, never tick down and cannot be dispelled.

A timed effect that is given no duration at all, by the ability or by the effect's own definition, also lasts the rest of the fight and from then on is treated as a lasting effect: nothing can dispel it. A duration as long as the fight's time limit (45 seconds, see Time limit and overtime) is shown as lasting the rest of combat, since it can never run out, but it still counts as timed and can still be dispelled.

A commander's talent bonuses are applied as timed effects at the start of combat with a duration equal to the time limit, so a dispel that removes buffs from an enemy would strip them; no ability or item dispels enemies at present. See How commanders work.

Duration and expiry

A fight advances in ticks of 0.1 seconds (see Combat). At the start of every tick, before any unit acts, each timed effect on each living unit loses 0.1 seconds. When nothing is left the effect is removed on the spot and the unit's stats are worked out again, so a stunned unit acts in the very tick its stun runs out.

  • The duration comes from the ability or trigger that applies the effect. If that gives none, the effect's own default duration is used.
  • Durations may differ by star level or commander level, see Scaling.
  • An effect applied in one tick starts counting down in the next, so it lasts its full duration.
  • If an expiring effect changed maximum health, current health is capped at the new maximum. Gaining maximum health also adds the same amount of current health.
  • Effects stay on a unit only while it lives. A unit that revives keeps its buffs and loses its timed debuffs.

Applying the same effect again

A unit holds at most one copy of each effect. Two effects are the same effect when they have the same identity, which for a named effect is the effect itself, whoever applied it. What happens on a second application depends on whether the effect stacks.

Effects that do not stack: refresh

Most effects have a single stack. Applying one again refreshes it:

  • Duration: the longer of the time left and the new duration. A refresh never shortens an effect, and durations do not add together.
  • Strength: the values of the new application replace the old ones. This is not a strongest wins rule: if a one star unit reapplies an effect that a three star unit applied, the weaker values take over.
  • Owner: the new applier becomes the source, so it gets the credit for later damage over time.
  • If the new application has no duration, the effect becomes lasting. An effect that is already lasting stays lasting.

Effects that stack

An effect with a maximum above one gains one stack per application, up to its maximum. With several stacks:

  • every number on the effect is multiplied by the stack count, including damage and healing per second;
  • on or off parts, such as a stun, are not multiplied;
  • the duration refreshes exactly as above, and all stacks share that one timer, so they all fall off together;
  • at the maximum, another application still refreshes the duration and the owner.

Different effects on one unit

Different effects never replace each other. Their stat changes are combined as described under Stat reference:

  • most stats simply add up (armour, magic resistance, attack speed, attack damage, maximum health, mana per second, critical strike chance, lifesteal, healing received, movement speed, mana gain, attack range);
  • evasion and damage reduction combine by multiplying what remains, so two sources of 50% give 75%, not 100% (see Damage, armour and resistance);
  • critical strike damage and revival health use the single highest value and ignore the rest;
  • a stun, silence, root, disarm or untargetable from any one effect is enough, and lasts until the last effect carrying it is gone.

Lasting effects from the same source

Lasting effects are counted once per source: one per alliance, per item and per aura ability. If two units carry the same aura ability, or you own two copies of the same global item, the bonus is applied once, not twice. Two different items or auras that grant the same stat do add up.

Auras

An aura is a lasting effect that an ability hands out when the fight is set up. It goes to the owner alone, to every ally, to every ally except the owner, or to every enemy.

  • An aura has no range. It reaches its whole side wherever the units stand.
  • It is handed out once. It does not end when its owner dies.
  • It cannot be dispelled and is not affected by silence or stun on its owner.
  • An aura may also hand out a trigger instead of, or as well as, a stat change.

Summoned units and lasting bonuses

A unit summoned during the fight is given the lasting bonuses that would have reached it had it been there from the start.

  • It receives everything that reaches its whole side: auras for every ally (and enemy auras aimed at its side), alliance steps and aces that affect every ally, and global items.
  • Of the bonuses an alliance gives only to its members, it receives just those of the alliances it belongs to itself. The alliances of the unit that summoned it do not matter. At present every summoned creature belongs to Conjurer and to nothing else, and placeables belong to no alliance, so the member bonuses of every other alliance pass them by.
  • Not passed on: an aura aimed only at its owner, an ace bonus for the ace alone, and an alliance bonus handed to randomly chosen members.

Conditions

A lasting effect can carry a condition. It then switches on and off during the fight as the condition becomes true or false. Conditions are checked at the start of every tick, before units act, against where units stand at that moment, and once more before the fight's first triggers.

  • Allies next to the unit: counts other living allies in the eight cells around it.
  • Enemies next to the unit: counts living enemies in the eight cells around it.
  • Allies within a radius: the same, out to a set number of cells.
  • Enemies within a radius: the same for enemies.
  • Outnumbered: the unit's side has fewer living units than the enemy side.
  • Outnumbering: the unit's side has more living units than the enemy side.
  • Distances count diagonals as one cell, so next to means any of the eight surrounding cells.
  • The unit never counts itself. Dead units and placeables (such as Spiked Palisade and Straw Decoy) never count; summoned creatures do.
  • A condition can be limited to units of one alliance, or to members of the alliance that grants the effect.
  • A condition needs a minimum count (one unless stated). Some effects instead apply once per unit counted, up to a cap; those behave like stacks.
  • Only lasting effects can be conditional. A condition on a timed effect is ignored and the effect is simply on for its duration.

Effects that grow with kills

A lasting effect can be scaled by the kills of the unit holding it. Its numbers are multiplied by the unit's kill count, up to a cap if it has one. With no kills it grants nothing. Each new kill during a fight raises the bonus at once. Only killing an enemy counts, and the kill goes to the unit that last damaged the victim.

  • Kills carry over from round to round and are counted for the unit itself for the whole match, whether or not it holds such an effect yet. A unit that already has kills gets the full bonus the moment it gains the effect, and the count is not lost when the effect is taken away.
  • When copies combine into a higher star, their kill counts are added together.
  • Kills made by summoned units are not kept after the fight.

Damage and healing over time

An effect can deal damage or restore health every second it is on a unit. The amount is given per second and is delivered in pulses every 0.5 seconds, each pulse worth 0.5 seconds of the rate. An effect of 100 per second therefore lands as 50 every 0.5 seconds.

  • The first pulse lands 0.5 seconds after the effect is applied, not at once. An effect that lasts a whole number of pulses delivers them all: its last pulse lands in the tick it expires.
  • Refreshing an effect does not restart its pulse timer, so reapplying a burn neither delays nor hastens the next pulse.
  • With stacks, the rate per second is multiplied by the stack count.
  • Damage over time has a damage type (magical unless stated) and is reduced by armour or magic resistance like any other damage. It can be absorbed by shields.
  • It is credited to the unit that applied (or last refreshed) the effect, which also gets the kill. The victim gains mana from it as from any damage.
  • It does not count as an attack: it cannot be evaded, cannot crit, gives no lifesteal and does not set off when damaged triggers. It can set off low health triggers.
  • Healing over time is raised or lowered by the target's healing received and by the heal dampening of overtime, and never heals above maximum health.
  • Overtime's damage amplification also applies to damage over time.
  • Any damage over time makes the whole effect a debuff. Healing over time alone leaves it a buff.

14 named effects deal damage over time, for example Burning Pitch, Shredded, Unravelling, Static Shock, Sentenced and 9 more. 1 named effect heals over time, for example Blood Offering. Damage over time is also part of the lasting bonus of Fevered Armlet. Healing over time is also part of the lasting bonus of Pact Retinue, Crowd Brawler, Kindling, Wildfire, Warding Cowl, Seer's Lens, Scrapkin.

Disables and other states

These are the on or off parts an effect can carry. The first four are the disables. A disable ends when the last effect carrying it expires or is dispelled; there is no reduction of disable durations and no diminishing returns.

Stun

A stunned unit does nothing of its own accord.

  • It cannot move, start an attack or start a cast.
  • An attack it was winding up is cancelled, and so is a cast it had begun. The cancelled attack does not use up its cooldown, and the cancelled cast spends no mana: the unit keeps its full mana and casts as soon as it can.
  • A step it was taking is paused and finishes after the stun.
  • An attack already in the air still lands.
  • Its attack cooldown and its ability cooldowns keep running while it is stunned, and it still gains mana from being hit.
  • Its triggers still fire, including timed ones, and it can still evade. It cannot make extra attacks.

Carried by 4 named effects: Stunned (units), Slammed, Stunned (items), Swept Aloft. Applied by Rivet Burst, Goring Charge, Grave Chain, Hellfire Brand, Hex Jar, Crushing Tide, Skyfall Arrow, Basilisk Glare, Stilled Hour, Pit Golem and 9 more.

Silence

A silenced unit cannot cast.

  • It cannot start a cast, whether the ability is paid for with mana or runs on a cooldown.
  • A cast it had begun is cancelled without spending mana or the cooldown.
  • It moves and attacks as normal and keeps gaining mana, so it casts the moment the silence ends.
  • Triggers, auras, item effects and alliance effects are not casts and keep working.

Carried by 6 named effects: Silenced (units), Pact Silence, Static Shock, Sentenced, Silenced (items), Hexed. Applied by Soul Harvest, Abyssal, Thunderhead Squall, Dread Sentence, Hushthorn Blade, Hexing Wand. Also part of the lasting bonus of Frenzy Visage.

Disarm

A disarmed unit cannot attack.

  • It cannot start an attack. An attack it was winding up is cancelled and does not use up its cooldown.
  • An attack already in the air still lands.
  • It still walks toward its target and still casts its ability.
  • It cannot make extra attacks.

Carried by 4 named effects: Disarmed (units), Disarmed (items), Last Light, Hexed. Applied by Riptide Spring, Picket Halberd, Lastlight Disc, Hexing Wand. Also part of the lasting bonus of Pact Retinue.

Root

A rooted unit cannot walk.

  • It cannot start a step. A step already under way is completed.
  • It can still attack and cast. While rooted it only picks targets inside its attack range; with none in range it waits.
  • A root does not stop a leap.

Carried by 2 named effects: Rooted (units), Rooted (items). Applied by Frost Sigil, Leeching Roots, Zephyr Staff.

Untargetable

Enemies cannot choose an untargetable unit. This is not a disable and not a debuff: on its own it counts as a buff. An effect that also carries a disable is a debuff all the same, and a friendly dispel ends it early: that is the case for Last Light, see Buffs, debuffs and disables.

  • Enemy attackers drop it and pick someone else; enemy abilities and triggers skip it, including area effects.
  • Attacks already in the air miss it.
  • Allies can still target it, for example to heal it.
  • It still suffers damage over time already on it and the damage of overtime.
  • It can still act, unless the same effect also stuns it.
  • A unit cannot cast at all while every enemy is untargetable.

Carried by 4 named effects: Skittering, Last Light, Swept Aloft, Smoke Cloud. Applied by Skitterling, Lastlight Disc, Squall Sceptre, Smoke and Mirrors, Smoke Bomb.

Surviving a killing blow

Once per fight, damage that would kill the unit leaves it at 1 health instead. It does nothing if the unit is already at 1 health or less. It sets off when it survives a killing blow triggers.

Carried by 1 named effect: Grave-Cloth. Applied by Gravecloth Mantle.

Revive

Not an on or off part but closely related: an effect can let its holder come back once per fight. Instead of dying the unit stays where it is with a share of its maximum health, loses its timed debuffs, and nobody is credited with a kill. With several such effects the highest share is used. A summoned unit whose time has run out does not revive. See when it revives.

Also part of the lasting bonus of Rebirth Sigil, Ashen.

There is no taunt, fear, sleep, blind or knockback in Warspire. A unit's choice of target is never forced by a status effect; see Combat for how targets are chosen.

Dispelling and immunity

The dispel effect strips timed effects from a unit.

  • Used on the caster or an ally it removes that unit's debuffs, disables included. Used on an enemy it removes that unit's buffs. An ability may instead say which to remove, or remove both.
  • It removes every matching effect at once, with all of its stacks.
  • It never removes lasting effects: alliance, item, ace and aura bonuses, or a timed effect that was given no duration.
  • Shields are not status effects and are not dispelled.
  • A revive removes the reviving unit's timed debuffs in the same way.

All 3 dispels in the game (Purging Light, Smoke and Mirrors, Nullfire Mantle) remove debuffs from the caster or its allies. Nothing at present removes buffs from an enemy.

There is no immunity to status effects. Nothing blocks a stun or a debuff from being applied, and a dispel gives no protection afterwards: the same effect can be applied again in the next tick. Effects described as immunity to a kind of damage work through damage reduction or resistance, not by refusing effects. The only way to avoid an effect is not to be chosen, which is what being untargetable does against enemies.

Scaling with star level and commander level

Any number on an effect, and its duration, may be a single value or one value per level. Units use their star level (one, two or three stars); a commander uses its player level in the same way.

  • A timed effect uses the level of the unit that applies it, not of the unit that receives it. A three star caster's slow is the three star slow on any target.
  • A lasting effect (alliance, item, aura, ace) uses the level of the unit that holds it. Two units of different stars under one alliance bonus can therefore get different amounts.
  • A trigger's chance, cooldown, interval and health threshold, and a targeting radius or count, use the level of the unit that owns the trigger.
  • If a list has fewer entries than the level, the last entry is used.
  • A refresh takes the values of the newest application, so the level of the latest applier decides.
