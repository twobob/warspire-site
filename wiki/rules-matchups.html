Matchups

Who you fight each round and when you meet a ghost.

Each round, when preparation ends, the game pairs up every player who is still alive. Each pair fights one fight, and both players see the same fight from their own side. Pairings are drawn fresh every round: there is no fixed schedule, no seeding by health or rank, and no league table. The draw is random, with a strong push away from opponents you met recently.

How opponents are chosen

  1. All living players are put in a random order.
  2. The first player in that order draws an opponent from everyone else who is not yet paired. Each candidate has a weight, and the chance of drawing a candidate is their weight divided by the sum of all the candidates' weights.
  3. Those two are paired and removed. The next unpaired player in the order draws from who is left, and so on.
  4. If the number of living players is odd, one player is left over at the end. That player fights a ghost.

Health, level, gold, streaks and whether a player is a bot play no part in the draw. Bots are paired exactly like people.

Avoiding recent opponents

The game remembers whom you fought in the last 2 rounds. A candidate you have not met in that time has a weight of 1. A recent opponent has a much smaller weight:

You fought them last round0.0520 times less likely than a fresh opponent
You fought them the round before last0.254 times less likely than a fresh opponent
Anyone else1the normal chance
  • The memory belongs to both players. If either of the two remembers the other, the smaller weight is used. The two weights are not multiplied.
  • If you met the same player in both of the remembered rounds, the smallest weight applies.
  • A recent opponent is never ruled out completely. When few players are left, rematches become common, and with two players left you fight each other every round.
  • A fight against a ghost is remembered by the player who fought it, as a meeting with the ghost's owner. The owner does not remember it.
  • Only the weights above matter. Opponents from longer ago are treated as fresh.

Ghosts

When an odd number of players is alive, the player left over by the draw has no partner. Instead of sitting the round out, they fight a ghost: a copy of another living player's warband.

  • The ghost's owner is picked at random from all the other living players, each with the same chance. The recent opponent weights do not apply to this pick, so the ghost can belong to someone you have just fought.
  • The ghost is the owner's board exactly as it stands when combat begins: the same units and stars in the same places, the same items, the same alliance bonuses, and their commander with its talents.
  • The owner is busy with a real fight of their own in the same round. They do not see the ghost fight.
  • If you lose to a ghost, you take player damage from its survivors exactly as if you had lost to the real player, and it counts as a loss for your streak.
  • If you beat a ghost, you get the win: victory gold and your win streak (Gold and income). The owner loses no health and their streak is untouched.
  • The owner gains nothing either way. A ghost that wins earns its owner no gold, no streak and no statistics, and kills made by the ghost are not added to the kill counts of the owner's real units, so they do not feed effects that grow with kills, such as Tallyknife, Trophy Cloak and Stoneborn.
  • A time out against a ghost damages only you.
  • Your record against a player counts ghost meetings too, and marks them as ghost meetings.

Any living player can be the one left over. Being the odd one out one round does not protect you from it the next round.

Sides

There is no home side and no away side. Every player sees their own warband on the near half of the board and the opponent's warband turned to face them, so the opponent's left flank stands opposite your right flank (Board and bench). Neither side has an advantage in the fight itself: units of the two sides act in turn, one from each side, and the side that goes first swaps with every step of the fight (a step is 0.1 seconds long). See Combat.

Eliminated players

  • A player who has been knocked out is never paired again and is never used as a ghost.
  • Players are knocked out only when a round is settled, after all its fights. So a pairing never loses a side in the middle of a fight.
  • A player who quits during combat still fights that round's fight with the board they left behind (forfeits).
  • A player who has disconnected but is still alive stays in the draw, and their board fights on without them (Matchmaking and lobbies).
  • When two players are left they fight each other every round until one falls.
