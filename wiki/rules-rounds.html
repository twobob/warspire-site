Rounds and phases

Preparation, combat, timers, loot rounds and neutral waves.

A match of Warspire is a string of rounds, and every round has the same two phases: preparation, when you shop and arrange your warband, and combat, when the warbands fight on their own. All 8 players move through the phases together. For the whole match from the lobby to the last player standing, see How a match works.

Preparation

Preparation is the one phase in which everything is open: buy and sell in the shop, reroll, buy experience, move units between the board and bench, equip items, and answer commander and talent offers. A timer counts down, and its length depends on the round.

RoundsPreparation time
Rounds 1 to 315 seconds
Round 4 onwards30 seconds
  • A loot round adds 10 seconds, so that you have time to choose an item.
  • Round 10, when you choose your commander, adds 10 seconds.

When the timer runs out, combat starts whether you are ready or not. At that moment the game tidies up for you:

  • An item offer you have not answered is settled by picking one of the offered items at random (Loot and items).
  • A commander offer you have not answered gives you the first commander shown, with its first build. An open talent choice is also made for you (How commanders work).
  • If automatic deployment is on, your strongest bench units are placed until the board is at its unit cap. If the board is over the unit cap, the units you touched most recently go back to the bench, and if the bench is full the remaining extra units are sold, cheapest first (Board and bench).

Readying up

The Ready button says you have finished. It does nothing on its own. Combat starts early, without waiting for the timer, at the moment every human player who is still alive and connected is ready.

  • Bots never hold the round up, and neither do players who have disconnected or been knocked out.
  • You can take your ready back at any time before combat starts.
  • Disconnecting clears your ready mark.
  • The mark is cleared at the start of every preparation, so you ready up again each round.
  • You cannot ready up during combat.
In a match where you are the only human, pressing Ready starts the fight at once. Readying up in the lobby before the match is a different thing: see Matchmaking and lobbies.

Combat

Every pairing fights at the same time (Matchups explains who meets whom). You cannot steer the fight; see Combat for how units behave.

A fight ends 1 second after one side has no units left, or when the time limit of 45 seconds is reached. From 35 seconds on, overtime makes units kill each other faster, so very few fights reach the limit. A fight that does reach the limit is a time out, which is bad for both sides (see player damage).

  • Summoned units count as units left, so a side with only summons standing is still in the fight. Placed objects such as wards and totems do not count: a side with nothing else left has been wiped out (Summons and placeables).
  • If the wiped out side gains a unit during the 1 second delay, the fight carries on, and the delay starts again from the beginning at the next wipe.
  • If the other side is wiped out as well before the delay is over, the fight is a draw (draws).
  • A side that brings no units to the fight loses at once, with no delay. If neither side brings any, the fight is a draw at once.

The combat phase lasts as long as the longest fight of the round, plus 10 seconds of padding. So combat lasts at most about 55 seconds, and is usually much shorter. The game does not tell you how long the phase will be until the last fight has ended, because that would give the results away. Once the last fight is over, every player sees the padding count down to the next shop.

While you wait

Your board is locked while your own fight runs. As soon as your own fight has ended you may rearrange the board again, even though other fights are still going: move units, put bench units on the board, or sell from the board. The shop, rerolls, buying experience and the bench stay open through the whole of combat. A unit bought during a fight goes to the bench, and it combines only with other bench copies until your board unlocks (Star upgrades). The fight you are watching always shows the boards as they were when combat began.

Items stay open too. At any time during combat you can equip an item, move it from one unit to another or take it off, on board units as well as bench units, even while your own fight is running. The fight in progress is not affected: the change counts from the next fight.

Three things wait for the next preparation: the Ready button, the commander choice and talent choices. If you buy a level during combat that unlocks a talent, the talent choice opens when the next preparation starts (How commanders work).

When the round ends

Nothing is settled while fights are still running. When the combat phase ends, the whole round is settled for everyone in one step, in this order:

  1. Every fight's result is applied. Losers take player damage, and win and loss streaks are updated.
  2. Players at 0 health or less are knocked out.
  3. Everyone still alive is paid gold and gains 1 point of experience.
  4. Copies that can combine do so (Star upgrades).
  5. Every shop that is not locked is refilled with new units, free of charge (The shop).
  6. The round number goes up by one and the next preparation starts.

This is why health totals on the scoreboard do not move during combat, and why your shop changes at the same moment as everyone else's.

Loot rounds

A loot round is an ordinary round with an item offer at the start of its preparation. Loot rounds are rounds 1 and 4, then every 4 rounds from round 8 (so rounds 1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28 and so on).

Every living player is shown 3 items and keeps one, with 1 reroll of the offer. The tier of the items on offer rises with the round number. Preparation is 10 seconds longer. The fight of a loot round is a normal fight against another player. Full details are on Loot and items.

RoundsItem tier offeredFirst loot round in this band
Rounds 1 to 7Tier 1Round 1
Rounds 8 to 15Tier 2Round 8
Rounds 16 to 23Tier 3Round 16
Rounds 24 to 31Tier 4Round 24
Round 32 onwardsTier 5Round 32

A player who already holds 12 unequipped items when the offer would open is not shown one, because there is no room to keep the item. Nothing is given in its place, and the longer preparation still runs. Equip an item before the loot round to make room (Loot and items).

Neutral rounds

Round damage is the part of player damage that depends only on the round number. Commander damage is what a surviving enemy commander adds. Standard means an ordinary fight with no offer.

The last round

A match cannot go past round 60. The limit exists because a fight between two empty boards is a draw, and a draw hurts nobody, so idle players could otherwise draw for ever. If more than one player is still alive when round 60 has been settled, the match ends there. The player with the most health wins, and the rest are placed in order of health (final placement). In practice matches end long before this.
