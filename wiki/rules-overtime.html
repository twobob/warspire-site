Time limit and overtime

What happens when a fight runs long.

A fight may last at most 45 seconds, which is 450 ticks of 0.1 seconds (how ticks work). So that fights are settled on the battlefield and not by the clock, overtime begins at 35 seconds: from then on every unit on both sides hits harder, heals less and loses health, a little more with each step.

What overtime does

Overtime starts 35 seconds into the fight and a new step begins every 1 second after that. A step begins at the very start of its tick, before any unit acts. Each step does three things, to both sides alike:

  • More damage. All damage is increased by 15% for each step so far. This covers attacks, abilities, damage over time, triggered effects and the overtime health loss itself. The increase is applied first, before armour, magic resistance and shields (Damage, armour and resistance). The bonus stops rising at 150%, reached at step 10.
  • Less healing. All healing is reduced by 10% for each step so far. This covers ability heals, healing over time and lifesteal. Shields are not healing and are not reduced. The reduction stops rising at 80%, reached at step 8.
  • Health loss. At the start of the step every living unit, placeables included, takes pure damage equal to a share of its own maximum health: 0.5% for each step so far, then raised by that step's damage bonus. Pure damage ignores armour and magic resistance, but shields still soak it. The share stops rising at 5%, reached at step 10.

The damage bonus and the healing reduction stay in force until the fight ends. They replace the values of the step before, they do not add to them. The overtime health loss gives no mana, and sets off no effects for being damaged. Effects that go off when a unit drops below a share of its health are the exception: overtime can set them off like any other loss of health. At present these are Lastlight Disc, Restorer's Reliquary, Crimson Reliquary, Smoke and Mirrors. If it finishes a unit, the kill goes to whichever enemy last damaged that unit. An effect that lets a unit survive a killing blow, or come back to life, still works against it.

Every step

There is room for 10 steps before the time limit. By the last of them all damage is increased by 150%, all healing is reduced by 80%, and a unit that has stood through every step has lost 56.38% of its maximum health to overtime alone, before a single enemy blow is counted.

Steps marked grace period only happen when one side is wiped out in the last moments and the fight runs on past the limit to settle the result.

How the fight is finally decided

The clock never picks a winner. A fight is decided in one of three ways (full rules):

  • Win. One side has no fighter left and still has none after the grace period of 1 second. This can run a little past the time limit if the last unit falls right at the end.
  • Draw. Both sides have no fighter left. With overtime taking health from everyone at the same moment, the last units of both sides can fall in the same tick.
  • Timeout. Both sides still have a fighter at 45 seconds. The fight is stopped where it stands. No winner is named, however uneven the remaining forces are.

What a timeout costs

A timeout counts as a loss for both players. Each player loses health as if the other had won: the units your opponent still has standing at the limit deal their damage to you, and your survivors deal theirs to your opponent. The sum is the usual one, set out on Player damage and elimination, so a side with more and stronger survivors still comes out ahead.

  • Both players have their win streak ended, and a player who took damage has their losing streak grow (Gold and income).
  • Against a ghost, only you take damage: a ghost has no health to lose.
  • In a neutral round (Rounds and phases) a timeout costs you the damage of the creatures still standing.
  • A draw is different: neither player takes any damage.
