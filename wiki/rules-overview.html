How a match works

A match from the lobby to the last player standing.

A match of Warspire is a series of rounds. In each round you prepare, your warband fights one rival, the loser loses health and everyone is paid. This page walks through the whole match in order; each step links to the page that covers it in depth, and the rules index lists them all. New players may prefer Getting started.

1. The lobby

You ask for a match and are seated in a lobby with up to 8 players. The match starts at once when every seat holds a player, or after a short wait from the latest arrival. Any seat still empty is taken by a bot, so a match always has 8 warbands. Matchmaking and lobbies covers the wait, leaving and rejoining.

2. The opening state

Every player starts with 5 gold, 100 health, level 1, an empty board and bench, and a first shop of 5 units. All players buy from one shared pool of units, so a unit your rivals hold is a unit you are less likely to see.

3. The preparation phase

Each round opens with preparation. It lasts 30 seconds (20 seconds in round 1, 15 seconds in rounds 2 and 3). Loot rounds (rounds 1 and 4, then every 4 rounds from round 8) add 10 seconds. Combat starts when the timer runs out, or sooner once every connected player has pressed ready. Rounds and phases has the full timetable.

Preparation is when you set up your board. The shop, experience, the bench and your items stay open during combat as well; only the board is locked, and only while your own fight runs. Choosing a commander, choosing a talent and pressing ready need the preparation phase. Board and bench lists what you may do during combat. The steps below are the things you do each round.

4. The shop

The shop shows 5 units and is refilled for free at the start of every round unless you lock it. A unit costs its tier in gold, from 1 to 5. A reroll costs 2 gold, and losing a round gives you one free reroll, kept until you use it. Which tiers appear depends on your level: at level 1 the shop holds only tier 1 and 2 units, and tier 5 first appears at level 8. Gilded Invitation makes your shop roll as if you were 1 level higher. See The shop and the Shop odds calculator.

5. Star upgrades

Owning 3 copies of the same unit at the same star combines them into one unit a star higher, up to 3 stars, which takes 9 copies in all. Selling a unit returns gold and puts its copies back in the pool. See Star upgrades. 8 of the 10 tier 5 units are aces, each tied to one alliance.

6. Levels and the unit cap

Your level sets how many units you may field: your level, and never fewer than 3. Veteran's Charter adds 1 to that cap. You gain 1 experience after every round, and may buy 5 experience for 5 gold at any time, in preparation or during a fight. The top level is 10. See Levels and experience.

7. Placing your warband

Your side of the board is 8 cells wide and 4 deep, and your bench holds 8 units. Only units on the board fight. If you leave space under your cap, your strongest benched units are placed for you when combat starts, unless you turn automatic placement off. If you are over the cap when combat starts, the units you touched most recently (moved, bought or given an item) go back to the bench; if the bench is full, the cheapest extra units are sold for their normal price. See Board and bench.

Different units on your board that share an alliance switch on its bonus at set counts; copies of the same unit count once. How alliances count explains the counting and the alliance list shows every step.

8. Loot rounds and neutral waves

On a loot round (rounds 1 and 4, then every 4 rounds from round 8) you are offered 3 items and keep one, with 1 reroll of the offer. If you have not chosen when combat starts, one is chosen for you. Later loot rounds offer higher item tiers. You hold up to 12 unequipped items, and a unit carries one. If you already hold 12 unequipped items, no offer is made, so equip items before a loot round. Most items are carried by one unit. 20 items are global: they work for your whole warband as soon as chosen and are never given to a unit. See Loot and items.

Neutral waves are switched off in the current rules, so every round is fought against a rival warband and loot arrives on the loot rounds alone. Rounds and phases lists both.

9. Choosing a commander

In the preparation phase of round 10 you are offered 4 commanders and choose one. That round's preparation is 10 seconds longer. The commander stands on your board as an extra fighter and does not count toward your unit cap, and can carry an item. If you do not choose, the first one offered is taken for you. At player levels 4, 6, 8 and 10 you pick a talent for it. See How commanders work.

10. Matchups

When combat starts the living players are paired at random, with a rival you met in the last 2 rounds much less likely. With an odd number of players, one of them fights a ghost: a copy of another player's warband. The ghost's owner is not affected by that fight. See Matchups.

11. Combat

Combat runs by itself in steps of 0.1 seconds. Units move toward enemies, attack, gain mana from attacking and from being hit, and cast their ability when the mana bar is full. Combat covers targets and movement, Damage, armour and resistance covers how hits are reduced, and Stat reference lists every stat.

Abilities and items apply buffs, debuffs and disables (How status effects work), fire on set events and pick targets by set rules (Triggers and targeting), and some create extra units for the fight (Summons and placeables).

A fight ends when one side is wiped out. From 35 seconds all damage rises and healing falls, and the fight is stopped at 45 seconds. See Time limit and overtime.

12. Damage to the loser

The loser of a fight loses health: the round number divided by 10, rounded down (so 1 from round 10, 2 from round 20), plus an amount for each enemy unit left standing, from 1 for a 1 star tier 1 unit to 4 for a 3 star tier 5 unit. A surviving commander adds 1, or 2 from round 31. Summons add nothing. The winner loses no health. If both sides are wiped out together the fight is a draw and nobody is hurt; if the time limit stops the fight, both sides count as losers and each takes the damage of the other's survivors. See Player damage and elimination.

13. Income, interest and streaks

After the fights every surviving player is paid: 7 gold (5 gold in rounds 1 and 2) for the round, 1 gold for a win, interest of 1 gold for every 10 gold you held when combat started, counting the win gold of that fight (up to 3, reached at 30 gold), streak gold, and gold from items such as Tithe Purse and Merchant's Compass. Gold you spend during the fight does not lower that round's interest. A win streak pays from 3 wins in a row and up to 4 gold at 8 in a row, after which the streak starts again; a losing streak pays from 3 losses in a row and up to 2 gold. A draw leaves both streaks as they are; a loss ends a win streak, but only counts toward the losing streak if you took damage. You can never hold more than 100 gold. See Gold and income.

Then the shop is refilled, the round number goes up and the next preparation phase begins.

14. Elimination and placement

A player whose health reaches 0 is out, and their units go back to the pool. Players are placed in the reverse order of going out; when several go out in the same round, the one with the lowest health takes the worst place. The last player standing wins. If more than one player is still alive after round 60, the match ends there and the survivors are placed by health. See Player damage and elimination.

15. After the match

The final standings are shown with each player's last warband and statistics for the whole match. See After the match. From there you can look for a new match.
