The shop

Shop size, tier odds by level, rerolls and free rerolls.

The shop is where you buy units. It shows 5 units at a time, drawn at random from the shared pool that all 8 players buy from. Which tiers it draws depends on your level; which units of a tier it draws depends on how many copies of each are still in the pool.

When the shop refreshes

  • You get your first shop when you join the match, before round 1.
  • After that, every player still in the match gets a new shop at the same moment: when the round ends for everyone. That is after the last fight of the round has finished and its results are applied, not when your own fight ends. If your fight ends early you keep the old shop, and can still buy from it or reroll it, until the round is over. See Rounds and phases.
  • The order at the end of a round is fixed: knocked out players leave (their units go back to the pool), income is paid, your copies combine, and then the new shop is rolled. So the new shop is rolled at your new level and against the pool as it stands after the round.
  • A reroll replaces the whole shop at once, at any time.
  • Buying a unit empties its slot. The slot stays empty until the next refresh or reroll.

Locking the shop

You can lock the shop. A locked shop is not replaced when the round ends, so the units in it are still there next round. The lock stays on until you turn it off: it does not release itself after one round. A reroll still replaces a locked shop. Locking does not reserve anything in the pool: another player can still buy the last copy of a unit that is sitting in your locked shop.

Tier odds by level

Each of the 5 slots is rolled on its own. First the slot rolls a tier with the chances below, then it picks a unit of that tier. Tier 3 (Rare) units first appear at shop level 3. Tier 4 (Epic) units first appear at shop level 6. Tier 5 (Legendary) units first appear at shop level 8.

Your shop level is your player level plus any item bonus. Gilded Invitation (+1) moves every shop roll up by that many rows of the table, at every level and not only at the top: with +1, a level 7 player rolls on the level 8 row. Player levels stop at 10, so the rows above that are reached only with such an item. The shop level never goes above 11 or below 1.

The level 11 row is not better than the level 10 row in every tier. It lowers tier 3 from 28% to 25% and tier 4 from 32% to 30%, and raises tier 1 from 12% to 15% and tier 2 from 18% to 20%.

To turn these into the chance of finding one particular unit, use the Shop odds calculator.

Which units can appear

  • Only shop units appear: 65 units (14 at tier 1, 14 at tier 2, 14 at tier 3, 13 at tier 4, 10 at tier 5). Summons are never sold.
  • A unit can only be offered while the pool still holds a copy of it. Within the rolled tier, each unit's chance is in proportion to its copies left in the pool, so a unit other players have bought heavily shows up less often.
  • Copies already drawn into the same shop are counted as taken while the rest of that shop is rolled. A shop can show the same unit more than once, but never more copies of it than the pool has.
  • A unit you already own at 3 star (on the board or the bench) is never offered to you, and any offers of it leave your shop the moment you complete it. If you sell that unit it can be offered again. See Star upgrades.
  • An offer in your shop does not hold a copy. If another player buys the last copy first, your offer cannot be bought.

When a tier runs dry

A tier counts as dry for you when no unit of it has a copy left in the pool, or when the only units with copies are ones you already own at 3 star. When a slot rolls a dry tier it does not come up empty. It moves to the nearest lower tier that has a unit to offer, going down one tier at a time to tier 1. If every lower tier is dry too, it goes up from the rolled tier one tier at a time to tier 5. This can show you a tier your level could not normally roll. A slot is left empty only when the whole pool has nothing you can be offered.

A tier that is merely thin is different: the chance of rolling the tier stays exactly as in the table, and the units of that tier that still have copies share it between them.

Rerolls

  • A reroll costs 2 gold and replaces all 5 slots, including empty ones.
  • You can reroll as often as you can pay for, in preparation and during combat.
  • A reroll avoids the units that were in the shop you just rerolled: within a tier it picks from other units when any other unit of that tier has copies, and only falls back to the old ones when nothing else is left. The automatic refresh at the end of a round has no such rule.
  • Units you did not buy are simply gone. Nothing goes back to the pool, because offers never took copies out of it.
  • Items can change the price: Recruiter's Ledger (-1 gold). The price never goes below 0 gold.

Free rerolls

  • When you lose a fight against another player (or their ghost, see Matchups), you are given one free reroll when the round ends. A fight that runs out of time counts as a loss for both sides, so both get one (see Time limit and overtime). A draw or a round without an opponent gives none.
  • While you hold a free reroll the reroll button reads FREE in place of the price, and your next reroll uses it up without costing gold.
  • An unused free reroll is kept from round to round until you spend it. You can never hold more than one: losing again while you still have one does not add a second.

Buying and the bench

A unit's price is set by its tier alone.

TierPrice in goldUnits in the shop
Tier 1 Common114
Tier 2 Uncommon214
Tier 3 Rare314
Tier 4 Epic413
Tier 5 Legendary510
  • A bought unit arrives as a 1 star and goes to the first free slot of your bench, which has 8 slots. You can also drag it straight onto the board when the board is open: during preparation, or during combat once your own fight has ended. You may place more units than your unit cap during preparation. When combat starts, the units you placed or moved most recently go back to the bench until the board is at the cap. If the bench is full, units still over the cap are sold instead, the cheapest on the board first. See Board and bench.
  • With a full bench a normal purchase is refused. There are two ways to buy anyway. You can drag the unit onto a free board cell while the board is open. Or the unit can complete a set: if you already hold 2 copies of that unit at 1 star that can combine right now, the purchase goes through and the 3 copies combine at once. See Star upgrades.
  • You can buy during combat. While your own fight is still running the unit can only go to the bench.
  • Selling gives gold back and returns the copies to the pool. Sell values are on Star upgrades.

Aces in the shop

8 units of tier 5 are aces: Spire Colossus, Mire King, Deep Basilisk, Hourblade, Abyssal Archon, Blood Matriarch, Soul Tyrant, Duskmother. Each has an ace effect tied to one alliance. When a slot draws from tier 5, an ace whose alliance is active on your board counts as if it had 15% more copies in the pool, so it is a little more likely to be the unit shown. The chance of rolling tier 5 itself does not change.
